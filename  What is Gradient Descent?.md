
# What is Gradient Descent?

<br>

Imagine you're standing on a mountain peak on a foggy morning, trying to reach the lowest point in the valley. You can't see far ahead, so you carefully feel the slope under your feet and take a small step downward. Step by step, you move in the direction where the ground feels steepest, hoping to eventually reach the bottom.

That's exactly what Gradient Descent does in AI/ML.  
It's an optimization algorithm used to minimize the cost function - basically, it tries to make the model's predictions as accurate as possible.

<br>

## Real-life analogies to understand Gradient Descent:.

<br>

### Climbing Down a Hill (in the Fog)  

Like I mentioned, you take small steps in the direction of the steepest descent - the gradient. If you take steps that are too big (large learning rate), you might trip and fall or even end up climbing a neighboring hill (overshooting). Too small, and you'll take forever to get down.

<br>

### Cooking by Tasting  


Say you're trying a new recipe and adjusting salt or spices gradually based on taste. Each iteration (taste and adjust) is like a gradient step - bringing you closer to the ideal flavor.

<br>

### Studying for Exams  

<br>
You solve mock tests, check your score, and identify where you went wrong. Next time, you study smarter, not harder - focusing on your weak spots. You're minimizing your "error function" with each round of practice.

<br>

### GPS Navigation  


Your GPS recalculates your route based on where you are and where you're heading - always adjusting your steps to reach the shortest path. Similarly, Gradient Descent updates model parameters to reach the minimum error.

Whether you're training a regression model or a neural network, Gradient Descent is at the heart of learning. It's simple, yet one of the most powerful concepts in machine learning.

**Fun fact:** Even large language models like ChatGPT learn using advanced forms of gradient descent during training.
